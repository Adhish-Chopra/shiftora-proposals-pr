Introduction and potential use cases

Potential use cases

Each area is a proposal to assess against current priorities, existing systems and vendor coverage. The relevant business team would define the operating rules and acceptance criteria.

01 Automation02 Complaints03 Loan readiness04 Product changes05 Invoices06 Deposit applications07 Loan closure
01

Central process automation

Resolve exceptions in existing automations

Automated work stops when an input is incomplete, inconsistent or outside the expected pattern.

Proposed output. Classify the issue, collect the missing evidence and prepare a recovery action for approval and resumption through the existing workflow.

Illustrative case

A supplier file arrives with a changed layout. The system identifies the affected fields, checks them against the expected record and prepares a corrected input for review.

Initial scope
One recurring exception type in one existing automation.
Measure
Handling time, correctly resolved cases and repeat failures.
Information needed
Approved process rules, sample inputs, run logs, exception queues and resolution history.
Bank control
The workflow owner approves recovery actions. Resubmission follows existing controls, including checks against duplicate processing.
02

Customer service and product operations

Carry complaints through to resolution

Resolving a complaint can require evidence and action from several bank teams or an external partner.

Proposed output. Assemble the case history, identify missing evidence, prepare the response and track the authorised correction or refund to completion.

Illustrative case

A customer is waiting for a refund. The system brings together the original transaction, partner response and credit status, then prepares the next action and customer explanation.

Initial scope
One payment or card complaint category.
Measure
Time to actual resolution, repeat contacts and reopened cases.
Information needed
Complaint records, transaction references, approved product terms and bank or partner status records.
Bank control
The bank retains complaint decisions, customer communications and payment authority. A closed ticket and a completed refund remain separate events.
03

Credit operations

Prepare loans for authorised disbursal

A sanctioned loan still needs complete evidence that its operational conditions have been met.

Proposed output. Build a condition-by-condition evidence checklist, flag missing or inconsistent records and route outstanding items to the responsible owner.

Illustrative case

A case has its approval letter but lacks a valid mandate or evidence for another condition. The system identifies exactly what is missing and prepares the follow-up.

Initial scope
One loan product and a defined set of sanction conditions.
Measure
Checklist accuracy, processing effort and readiness turnaround.
Information needed
Sanction conditions, bank-approved checklists, supporting documents and mandate or registration status.
Bank control
Credit judgement, verification and disbursal authority remain with the bank. The output supports the reviewer’s decision.
04

Product, technology and testing

Verify product changes before release

An approved fee, reward or repayment change must produce consistent results across the affected customer journey.

Proposed output. Translate approved rules into reviewable test scenarios, compare expected and actual results, and produce a traceable defect report.

Illustrative case

A reward rule changes how a reversed purchase is treated. Test cases cover the purchase, refund and reward reversal, including boundary dates and amounts.

Initial scope
One product change in an approved test environment.
Measure
Scenario coverage, test preparation effort and confirmed defects.
Information needed
Approved specifications, calculation examples, existing test cases and test-environment outputs.
Bank control
The product owner approves expected results; the testing team reviews evidence and retains release sign-off.
05

Finance and procurement

Complete invoice exception work

An invoice can remain blocked while a branch, supplier and approver resolve missing acceptance or disputed charges.

Proposed output. Bring together the invoice, contract and acceptance evidence; identify the blocker and prepare the follow-up needed to return the case to the payment process.

Illustrative case

An invoice bills the full service period, but only part has been accepted. The system assembles the supporting records and routes the discrepancy for an authorised decision.

Initial scope
One supplier or service category.
Measure
Manual handling effort, exception age and avoidable rework.
Information needed
Sample invoices, purchase or contract records, acceptance evidence and approved workflow status.
Bank control
Finance and procurement retain acceptance, accounting and payment approval. Existing Oracle and RPA coverage is checked before choosing the additional work.
06

Deposit operations and partnerships

Complete funded deposit applications

An application can involve separate payment, verification and deposit-activation states across the bank and a partner.

Proposed output. Trace the application across those states, identify pending work and route the authorised completion or refund with a clear customer update.

Illustrative case

The customer has paid but verification is incomplete. The system identifies the current blocker, prepares the required action and tracks the resulting activation or refund.

Initial scope
One partner journey with approved operating rules.
Measure
Pending-case age, completion accuracy and customer follow-ups.
Information needed
Application identifiers, payment references, verification status, activation records and approved channel rules.
Bank control
Bank officials retain identity verification and account-opening decisions. Refunds and customer communications follow the approved process.
07

Loan servicing and credit operations

Finish loan closure and release tasks

Repayment is followed by several closure tasks, each with its own evidence, owner and completion status.

Proposed output. Track approved adjustments, closure documents, bureau updates and security-release tasks; prepare follow-ups and escalate outstanding items.

Illustrative case

Repayment is confirmed, but a closure document or release task remains pending. The system identifies the owner and the evidence needed to finish it.

Initial scope
One secured loan product.
Measure
End-to-end closure time, overdue tasks and repeat requests.
Information needed
Repayment confirmation, closure instructions, task records and document or release status.
Bank control
The bank determines closure eligibility and authorises all adjustments, releases and reporting updates.

Working with Unity’s current platforms

For Unity, the proposed work would sit alongside Oracle Fusion, Oracle analytics and existing RPA workflows, using approved interfaces or sample exports. Current applications remain the source of record, with business decisions and approvals retained by the bank.

Proposed next step

A focused two-week proof of concept

Choose one workflow with the central process automation team. Over two working weeks, produce a working demonstration and a measured assessment of its value to the process owner.

The two-week period starts once scope, the process owner and approved sample access are agreed.

Days 1-3

Define the case and baseline

Select one workflow and agree its boundaries. Review approved sample cases, current system coverage and the measures that will determine whether the output is useful.

Days 4-7

Build the working demonstration

Connect the approved sample inputs, implement the agreed logic and produce the actual reviewable output: an evidence pack, prepared action or test report.

Days 8-10

Evaluate with the process owner

Compare the output with the current process on routine and difficult cases. Record accuracy, handling effort, remaining exceptions and the changes needed for implementation.

What Unity receives

  • A working demonstration on an agreed, bounded set of cases.
  • A comparison of output quality and handling effort against the current process.
  • A clear recommendation on scope, dependencies and the next implementation step.

Evaluation would use approved sample data and access arrangements. Production deployment would follow the bank’s applicable reviews and a separately agreed implementation scope.
